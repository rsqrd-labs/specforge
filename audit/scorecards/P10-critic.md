| 9 | Variable interpolation safety | 4 | No escaping issues (plain string concatenation via a list + `"\n".join`, `critic.py:181-201`) — safe by construction, no `.format()`/brace-collision risk (contrast P14's finding). | 10 | Consistency with target model | 4 | Same unverified-per-tier concern as the generation family — the critic itself always runs on the cheap judge tier regardless of which tier generated the artifact (by design), so a strong-tier-generated artifact is graded by a weaker model, which is an intentional cost trade-off the codebase has clearly made consciously (module docstring, cost comments) rather than an oversight. |
| 11 | Token & cost efficiency | **2** | `_build_critic_user_prompt` (`critic.py:181-201`) passes the **full, untruncated** `artifact_text` and **every** dependency's **full** content with no length cap, no `_compact_text`-style budget, and no per-stage size limit — directly contrasting `online_eval.py`'s `_PROMPT_LIMITS`/`_COMPACT_RETRY_LIMITS` (P14), which exists precisely because the same class of artifact (a multi-file HARNESS, or a fully-populated TASKS.md with 20-50 tasks) can be tens of thousands of tokens. Since the critic runs on **every** generation (not just on-demand eval), and doubly so under the legacy synchronous path where it sits on the critical path before `done`, an uncapped harness/tasks artifact sent to "the cheap judge model" (module comment, `critic.py`) risks defeating the cost rationale the comment states, and — for a genuinely enormous harness — risks the judge call itself erroring on context/output limits, which the fail-open handler would silently swallow into a `passed=True` (i.e., the quality gate goes quiet exactly when the artifact is largest and most in need of grading). |
| 12 | Determinism & robustness | 4 | `MAX_REGENERATES = 1` (`critic.py:35`) bounds the legacy synchronous retry loop; `_extract_json_object` (`critic.py:204-224`) tolerates Markdown-fenced or preamble-prefixed judge output, a realistic robustness accommodation for judge-model quirks. |
| 13 | Maintainability | 4 | Deliberately not Langfuse-sourced, with the security rationale stated directly in the module docstring (`critic.py:7-10`) — consistent, considered stance shared with P08/P15. No version constant analogous to `STAGE_PROMPT_VERSIONS`, so a critic-prompt edit isn't tracked in cost-ledger telemetry the way stage-prompt edits are. |
| 14 | Testability & eval coverage | 3 | No `harness/prompt_eval` grader or golden-corpus entry targets critic behavior directly (the golden corpora found are for route/compression/grounding, not critic verdicts) — a regression that made the critic systematically too lenient or too strict would only surface via user reports or the eval-batch's own scoring (a different, separately-maintained judge), not a dedicated critic-quality test. |

**Top risk (dimension 11):** unbounded artifact+dependency length is a genuine, reproducible cost/robustness gap that scales with exactly the artifacts most likely to be large (harness, tasks) and runs on every single generation, not just an opt-in eval path.

**Verdict:** HIGH.
